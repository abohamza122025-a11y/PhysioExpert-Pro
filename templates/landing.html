Stroke (Hemiplegia)

Stroke (Hemiplegia) Print
Clinical Presentation & Definition

Hemiplegia refers to unilateral paralysis or severe weakness resulting from a stroke, typically affecting one side of the body opposite to the side of the brain lesion. It manifests as impaired motor control, spasticity, sensory deficits, balance issues, and often cognitive or perceptual impairments, significantly impacting functional independence.

⚡
Electrotherapy
Type: Functional Electrical Stimulation (FES)
PARAMETERSFrequency: 20-50 Hz; Pulse Duration: 200-300 µs; On/Off Ratio: 1:3 to 1:5; Ramp up/down: 1-2 sec; Intensity: To achieve a strong, non-painful contraction or functional movement.
Goal: Facilitate muscle contraction, promote motor learning and neuroplasticity, improve strength and range of motion, reduce spasticity, prevent disuse atrophy, and improve functional task performance (e.g., gait, reaching).
∿
Ultrasound
Type: Therapeutic Ultrasound (Pulsed / Continuous)
PARAMETERSFrequency: 1 MHz (deep) or 3 MHz (superficial); Mode: pulsed 20-50% (acute/painful) or continuous (chronic tightness); Intensity: 0.5-1.0 W/cm²; Duration: 5-8 min per area with a moving transducer; 3-5 sessions/week. Use lower intensity over areas of impaired sensation.
Goal: Adjunct for hemiplegic shoulder pain and periarticular soft-tissue tightness: reduce pain, increase tissue extensibility before stretching and mobilization to improve range of motion.
🏃 Therapeutic Exercises
Early Mobilization and Foundational Motor Re-education (Acute/Early Sub-acute)

Passive Range of Motion (PROM)

: Carefully move all affected joints through their full available range of motion, ensuring no pain or undue stress. Focus on the shoulder (to prevent subluxation and pain), elbow, wrist, hip, knee, and ankle.

(10-15 repetitions per joint, 2-3 times per day.)

Assisted Active Range of Motion (AAROM)

: Encourage the patient to initiate movement with the paretic limb while the therapist or unaffected limb provides assistance to complete the range. Focus on movements with early motor return, such as shoulder flexion, elbow flexion/extension, and ankle dorsiflexion.

(10-15 repetitions per movement, 2-3 times per day.)

Bridging (Assisted)

: From a supine position with knees bent and feet flat, instruct the patient to lift their hips off the bed. Provide manual assistance at the pelvis as needed. Focus on gluteal and hamstring activation, and trunk stability.

(10-15 repetitions, 2-3 times per day, holding for 3-5 seconds if possible.)

Bed Mobility: Rolling

: Practice rolling from supine to side-lying towards both the affected and unaffected sides. Use visual cues, upper limb movements, and lower limb push-off as appropriate. Progress to independent rolling.

(5-10 repetitions per side, 2-3 times per day.)

Supported Sitting Balance

: Progress from sitting with full trunk support to unsupported sitting, initially with feet supported. Focus on maintaining an upright posture, head control, and reacting to small perturbations. Encourage weight shifting within the base of support.

(5-15 minutes, 2-3 times per day, gradually increasing duration and decreasing support.)
